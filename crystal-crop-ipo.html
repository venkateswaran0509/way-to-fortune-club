Crystal Crop Protection IPO: Growth Story or Acquisition Risk?

Crystal Crop Protection is entering the stock market with a ₹355 crore fresh issue and an offer for sale of up to ₹100.71 crore. The IPO price band is ₹259–₹272 per share, with a lot size of 55 shares.

But the bigger story is not just the IPO. It is the company's proposed ₹2,268 crore acquisition of FMC India’s crop protection business.

What does Crystal Crop Protection do?

Crystal Crop Protection manufactures and sells agricultural products such as herbicides, fungicides, insecticides, natural crop solutions and seeds under the Crystal and Saffire brands.

The company operates six crop protection manufacturing plants and three seed processing units, supported by a distribution network of 14,717 active distributors.

Its business has shown consistent growth, with revenue increasing from ₹2,230 crore in FY24 to ₹3,152 crore in FY26. Net profit also improved from ₹87 crore to ₹145 crore during the same period.

Financial performance: Growth with a few concerns

The company has demonstrated revenue and profit growth, while its gross margin improved from 34.29% in FY25 to 38.49% in FY26.

However, its FY26 net profit margin was approximately 4.6%, which indicates relatively thin profitability.

Another point to monitor is cash flow. Net operating cash flow declined from ₹383 crore in FY25 to ₹256 crore in FY26, although it remained around 1.8 times the reported net profit.

Borrowings stood at ₹952 crore as of June 2026, while the reported net debt-to-equity ratio was 0.41 times.

The biggest question: FMC India acquisition

Crystal Crop Protection plans to acquire FMC India's crop protection business for approximately ₹2,268 crore, including the upfront consideration and intellectual property licence fee.

The acquisition could strengthen its product portfolio and market presence. However, the funding structure remains an important concern.

The company has indicated that the acquisition will be funded through a combination of debt and equity, but the exact funding split has not been disclosed.

For perspective, the IPO proceeds include ₹247.94 crore for repayment of borrowings and ₹22.06 crore for investment in its subsidiary, Saffire. Together, these account for approximately 76% of the fresh issue.

Investors should therefore monitor how the much larger FMC acquisition affects the company's debt and financial position.

Key risks investors should know

There are several factors worth examining before evaluating the IPO:

  • Thin profit margins: FY26 net profit margin was approximately 4.6%.
  • Acquisition funding: The exact debt-equity funding mix for the FMC deal is not yet disclosed.
  • Audit qualifications: The company has received qualified audit opinions relating to a ₹16.30 crore insurance claim receivable dating back to FY2012-13.
  • Pending legal proceedings: These include a CBI case involving alleged fraudulent claims of ₹75.53 crore in TED refunds, a DGFT demand of ₹109.42 crore and an ED attachment of approximately ₹30.2 crore in fixed deposits. These matters remain subject to legal proceedings.
  • Industry competition: A significant part of its portfolio consists of generic, off-patent molecules, where pricing competition can affect margins.

Valuation: Is the IPO reasonably priced?

At the upper price band, the IPO is valued at approximately 24.6 times earnings, compared with a simple peer average of around 20 times.

The company's return on net worth is reported at 7.14%, which makes the premium valuation an important consideration.

While revenue and profit growth are encouraging, investors should also assess profitability, acquisition execution, debt levels and the resolution of pending legal matters.

Final takeaway

Crystal Crop Protection has a growing business, an established distribution network and an opportunity to expand through the FMC India acquisition.

However, the acquisition's funding structure, audit qualifications, pending legal proceedings and valuation make this an IPO that deserves careful evaluation.

The key question is not just how much the company can grow, but how it will finance that growth without putting pressure on its balance sheet.
