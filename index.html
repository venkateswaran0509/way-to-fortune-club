01 / THE CONCEPT

What does the ratio mean?

Think of it as a price comparison. If one gram of gold costs ₹12,000 and one gram of silver costs ₹150, divide the gold price by the silver price. The answer is 80. That means 80 grams of silver have roughly the same market value as one gram of gold.

Illustrative example · Not today's prices
₹12,000 ÷ ₹150
1g gold = 80g silver in value
80 : 1

The ratio describes a relationship between prices. It does not predict which metal will rise or fall.

04 / HOW TO READ IT

High ratio. Low ratio.

Higher ratio

Silver is less expensive relative to gold than it is at a lower ratio. Example: 100 grams of silver equal one gram of gold in value.

Lower ratio

Silver is more expensive relative to gold than it is at a higher ratio. Example: 50 grams of silver equal one gram of gold in value.

A high ratio is not automatically a buy signal. A low ratio is not automatically a sell signal.

05 / THINK CRITICALLY

What it helps with — and what it doesn't

What it helps with

  • Compare relative prices of gold and silver.
  • Understand how two precious metals can behave differently.
  • Put today's relationship into historical context.
  • Add one data point to broader market research.

Where it falls short

  • It cannot predict future prices or returns.
  • Silver can be more volatile than gold.
  • Industrial demand can influence silver prices.
  • It excludes retail premiums, taxes and transaction costs.
  • Historical patterns may not repeat.
06 / BEFORE YOU ACT

Important warnings

01
A high ratio does not mean “buy silver now”.

The ratio can stay high or move higher. It is not a timing signal.

02
Compare like with like.

Check purity and use comparable spot-price references. Jewellery prices are different from spot prices.

03
Include the real-world costs.

GST, making charges, dealer margins, storage and buy/sell spreads affect the actual outcome.

04
Do not rely on one indicator.

Interest rates, currency movements, inflation expectations and industrial demand also matter.
