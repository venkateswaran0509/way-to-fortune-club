Mahanadi Coalfields IPO 2026: Should You Invest? Business Analysis, Financials, Valuation and Risks

India's coal sector is getting another major listing, and this time it is Mahanadi Coalfields Limited (MCL), one of the country's largest coal-producing companies.

The company accounts for approximately 21% of India's domestic coal production. It has substantial coal reserves, strong profitability and the backing of Coal India Limited.

At first glance, these numbers may make the IPO look attractive.

But there are some important questions investors should consider:

  • Is the company's profitability growing or declining?
  • How dependent is it on thermal power generation?
  • Will renewable energy affect its future business?
  • Is the IPO valuation attractive?
  • What are the risks disclosed in the prospectus?

Let's understand the Mahanadi Coalfields IPO by examining its business model, financial performance, valuation and key risks.

The IPO is entirely an Offer for Sale (OFS).

This is an important distinction.

In a fresh issue, a company issues new shares and receives the money raised to fund its business activities, repay debt or support expansion.

In an OFS, existing shareholders sell their shares to the public.

In this IPO, Coal India is selling up to 66.18 crore shares, representing approximately 10% of Mahanadi Coalfields.

Therefore, Mahanadi Coalfields will not receive the IPO proceeds. The money will go to Coal India, the selling shareholder.

2. What Does Mahanadi Coalfields Actually Do?

Mahanadi Coalfields Limited was incorporated in 1992 and operates primarily in Odisha.

It is a wholly owned subsidiary of Coal India Limited before this public offering.

Its major operations are concentrated in two coalfields:

  • Talcher Coalfield
  • Ib Valley Coalfield

The company's primary business is mining and supplying non-coking coal.

Non-coking coal is mainly used for electricity generation and industrial applications.

Its major customers include:

  • State and central power utilities
  • Independent power producers
  • Captive power plants
  • Cement manufacturers
  • Sponge iron manufacturers
  • Other industrial consumers

In simple terms, Mahanadi Coalfields extracts coal from its mines and supplies it to power plants and industries that need coal for their operations.

How large is the company?

According to its FY26 disclosures:

  • Coal production: 218.31 million tonnes
  • Share of India's domestic coal production: Approximately 21%
  • Audited coal reserves: 9,840.31 million tonnes

The company is India's largest single coal-producing company by production in FY26.

One particularly interesting number is its reserve base.

At the FY26 production level, its audited reserves could support approximately 45 years of production.

However, this estimate assumes production remains at the current level. The prospectus also notes that if production grows at a historical CAGR of 4.70%, the existing audited reserves would support approximately 25 years.

So, reserve life should not be interpreted as a guaranteed 45-year business runway.

4. What Are the Company's Biggest Strengths?

A. Strong market position

Mahanadi Coalfields contributes approximately 21% of India's domestic coal production.

Its scale gives it an important position in India's coal supply chain.

B. Substantial coal reserves

The company has audited coal reserves of approximately 9,840 million tonnes.

This provides significant resource visibility, although actual mine life depends on production growth, approvals and future demand.

C. Low-cost mechanised operations

The RHP highlights low-cost, mechanised mining operations as one of the company's competitive advantages.

Efficient mining operations can help the company maintain profitability and compete effectively.

D. Support from Coal India

Mahanadi benefits from the strategic and operational support of Coal India Limited.

This includes access to group-level expertise, infrastructure and management resources.

E. Strong profitability

Despite the recent decline, the company continues to report substantial profits and healthy operating margins.

Its FY26 net profit was approximately ₹10,698 crore.

These are important strengths to consider when evaluating the business.

5. Key Risks Investors Should Not Ignore

A strong company can still be a risky investment if its business model faces structural challenges.

Here are some important risks disclosed in the RHP.

Risk 1: Heavy dependence on thermal power

This is one of the biggest long-term risks.

In FY26, approximately 68.62% of the company's sale of products went to the power generation sector, including state and central generation companies and independent power producers.

Another 16.57% went to captive power plants.

This means a substantial portion of its business is connected to coal-based power generation.

As India increases its renewable energy capacity, the long-term demand outlook for thermal coal could change.

The RHP itself acknowledges this risk.

More importantly, the impact is not purely theoretical.

The company's sales of products to the power sector declined by 4.82% in FY26 compared with FY25. The prospectus attributes this decline primarily to a higher contribution from renewable energy sources to the power grid.

This does not mean coal demand will disappear immediately. Thermal power continues to play an important role in India's electricity supply.

However, investors should consider the long-term energy transition when valuing a coal business.

Risk 2: Customer concentration

Mahanadi Coalfields depends on a relatively small group of large customers.

In FY26, its top 10 customers contributed approximately 55.94% of revenue from operations.

Its largest customer alone accounted for 14.64%.

Government-owned and controlled power generation companies and utilities contributed 44.81% of revenue from operations in FY26.

This concentration creates risks.

If major customers reduce their coal purchases, delay payments or change procurement arrangements, the company's revenue and cash flow could be affected.

Risk 3: Dependence on contractors

Mining operations require machinery, manpower, transportation and other specialised services.

Mahanadi relies on third-party contractors for a substantial portion of its operating activities.

In FY26, its top 10 contractors accounted for approximately 60.02% of contractual expenses.

Any disruption in contractor services could affect production, increase costs or delay mining operations.

Risk 4: Environmental and regulatory approvals

Coal mining is subject to environmental regulations, forest clearances and several government approvals.

The company needs these approvals for mine expansion and new mining projects.

Delays or restrictions could affect its production growth plans.

The RHP also identifies additional environmental appraisal requirements for mines in the Ib Valley industrial cluster, which is classified as a Severely Polluted Area.

This is an important operational risk because future expansion depends partly on timely approvals.

Risk 5: Legal proceedings

The latest RHP discloses the following proceedings against the company:

  • 49 criminal proceedings
  • 111 tax proceedings
  • 52 actions taken by statutory or regulatory authorities

The aggregate quantified amount involved in these proceedings is approximately ₹11,812.67 crore.

This figure represents amounts involved in pending proceedings to the extent they are ascertainable and quantifiable.

It should not be interpreted as a confirmed loss, conviction or an amount that the company must immediately pay.

However, the number and scale of these proceedings deserve attention.

Risk 6: Contingent liabilities

As of September 30, 2026, the company disclosed contingent liabilities of approximately ₹12,513.29 crore.

These are potential obligations whose actual occurrence or financial impact depends on uncertain future events.

They are not the same as confirmed debt or an immediate payment obligation.

The prospectus states that these contingent liabilities represented approximately 55.86% of the company's net worth.

Investors should understand the nature of these obligations and the possibility of their materialising.

6. Mahanadi Coalfields IPO Valuation: Is the Price Attractive?

Now comes one of the most important questions: Is the IPO valuation reasonable?

The upper end of the IPO price band is ₹136 per share.

The company's FY26 diluted EPS is ₹16.13.

Using these figures:

P/E Ratio = IPO Price / FY26 EPS

₹136 / ₹16.13 = approximately 8.43 times.

Therefore, the IPO is priced at approximately 8.43 times its FY26 earnings.

At first glance, this may appear inexpensive because it is a single-digit P/E valuation.

But a low P/E ratio does not automatically mean a stock is undervalued.

Investors should also consider:

  • Whether earnings are growing
  • Whether operating margins are improving
  • Whether the business has long-term growth opportunities
  • Whether the industry faces structural challenges
  • Whether the current valuation already reflects the company's strengths and risks

Mahanadi has strong production capacity and substantial reserves.

However, its revenue, operating EBITDA and net profit have declined between FY24 and FY26.

That makes future earnings growth an important factor in evaluating the valuation.

The IPO price should be assessed in the context of both its current profitability and its long-term business outlook.

7. What About Dividends?

Coal India is known for its dividend payments, but investors should not automatically assume that Mahanadi Coalfields will follow the same dividend pattern.

Mahanadi's own dividend history and future dividend policy should be examined independently.

Dividend payments depend on the company's profitability, cash requirements, capital expenditure, board decisions and applicable regulations.

There is no guarantee that shareholders will receive a particular dividend or dividend yield after listing.

Therefore, investors should not apply solely based on expectations of high dividend income.

8. What Is the Objective of This IPO?

The IPO is a complete Offer for Sale by Coal India.

There is no fresh issue component.

Coal India is offering approximately 10% of its holding in Mahanadi Coalfields to public investors.

The company itself will not receive the proceeds.

Following the offer, Coal India is expected to retain approximately 90% ownership.

This is primarily a stake dilution and divestment transaction rather than a fundraising exercise for Mahanadi's expansion.

9. Final Analysis: Should You Consider the Mahanadi Coalfields IPO?

Mahanadi Coalfields is undoubtedly a large and strategically important coal producer.

It has several strengths:

  • Approximately 21% share of India's domestic coal production.
  • Substantial audited coal reserves.
  • Strong profitability.
  • Low-cost mechanised mining operations.
  • Strategic support from Coal India.
  • Significant presence in India's thermal power supply chain.

But there are also important concerns:

  • Revenue and profit declined over FY24–FY26.
  • Operating EBITDA margins have moderated.
  • A substantial portion of sales depends on thermal power generation.
  • Renewable energy growth creates a long-term structural challenge.
  • Revenue is concentrated among a limited number of large customers.
  • Contractor dependence, environmental approvals, legal proceedings and contingent liabilities need attention.

The latest half-year results show a recovery in revenue and net profit, but investors need to see whether this improvement can continue.

At approximately 8.43 times FY26 earnings, the IPO is not necessarily expensive based on the P/E ratio alone. But the company's future growth prospects and structural risks must also be considered.

My Personal View

I am personally not applying for this IPO.

My concern is not that Mahanadi Coalfields is a weak business. It is a large, profitable company with a strong position in India's coal industry.

My concern is whether the current valuation adequately compensates investors for the company's slowing historical growth and long-term dependence on thermal coal.

I would rather evaluate its performance as a listed company and observe how its earnings, margins and business outlook develop.

Remember, a good company does not automatically mean a good IPO at every price.

And every IPO does not guarantee listing gains.

Investors should analyse the business, valuation and risks instead of following market hype.
